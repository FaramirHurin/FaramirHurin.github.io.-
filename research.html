Research

My research spans multiple aspects of AI security, including secure code generation, prompt injection and jailbreaking, privacy, poisoning, and evasion. AI security: software engineering, fraud detection, federated learning, face recognition, and LLMs.

Current work

  • LLM and agent security. Prompt injection in agent skills and skill marketplaces, jailbreaking attacks against aligned models, and, with the École de technologie supérieure in Montréal, an inference-time method that guides models toward safer and higher-quality output by inserting structured comments in their context.
  • Federated learning. Backdoors that persist through federated aggregation with minimal attacker participation, in contrastive learning, where the lack of labeled data makes the setting particularly challenging for attackers.
  • Face recognition and privacy. Physical protections such as face painting, partly evaluated with the Royal Military Academy in Brussels, and synthetic datasets for training and benchmarking recognition models, including how much they leak about the real faces they were built from.
  • Next. The security of the supply chain through which open-weight models reach the organizations that download, fine-tune and deploy them.
